Platform Roadmap · Executive Overview

Three phases to a workshop that runs itself

Where the platform is today, the three steps to where it needs to be, and the four decisions only you can make.

The problem is not that we lack a system. It is that the system records work rather than governing it.

A car can be worked on before the customer has agreed to pay for it. No single person owns who touches which vehicle. Nothing stops a car leaving before it has been checked or billed. And the customer themselves is outside the system entirely — every question they have arrives as a phone call to a branch.

The roadmap closes those gaps in three phases, in the order that each one makes the next one possible. Phase 1 is largely built, and it now carries the mobile app with it — but a promotional app, not a transactional one. An app that shows a customer their live job card has to sit on top of a floor that is already running properly, so those features wait. An app that tells them who we are and puts our number one tap away does not have to wait for anything.

The three phases

Each phase leaves the business working on its own. Nothing is half-migrated at a phase boundary.

PHASE 01
Now
LARGELY BUILT

Control the job, and put Pioneer on the phone

No work begins without the customer’s agreement, every car has one named person answerable for it, and Pioneer has a presence customers keep on their home screen.

What changes
  • Estimate goes to the customer as a link — no login needed
  • They approve or reject each line individually
  • The job card opens by itself once everything is approved
  • A Team Leader assigns the technician and books the start and finish time
  • One screen for him: his people, his cars, their hours, who is free
  • The technician starts, pauses and completes on his own screen
  • A promotional app on iOS and Android — who we are, what we do, current offers, and how to find or call a branch
Why it matters
  • Ends the “I never approved that” invoice argument
  • 171 cars are waiting right now with nobody responsible
  • A promised delivery date becomes a booked slot, not a guess
  • Booked hours versus actual hours becomes measurable
  • A marketing channel we own outright, with no per-message cost

The app is promotional only, on purpose It shows information and it makes a phone call. It takes no bookings, no complaints and shows no job card status — all three need the customer to sign in, and customer sign-in is a build of its own. Keeping those out is what turns the app from a three-month project into a few weeks, and it means nothing on the shop floor can be touched from a phone.

PHASE 02
Next
AWAITING A DECISION

Close the loop

Nothing leaves the workshop unchecked, unbilled or unrecorded.

What changes
  • Approved parts raise a requisition against stores automatically
  • A quality check and road test before the car is released
  • A gate pass the guard checks — temporary, then final after invoicing
  • A Quality Coordinator role to own that step
Why it matters
  • Parts consumption stops being reconciled after the fact
  • Comebacks get caught inside the workshop, not at the gate
  • A car physically cannot leave on an unpaid job

The business gain Parts leakage and comeback rate both become numbers you can manage.

PHASE 03
Later
BLOCKED EXTERNALLY

Connect outward

Stop typing the same information into two systems.

What changes
  • Invoices push straight into ORBIT at close-out
  • The customer gets WhatsApp updates, not just email
  • The punch machine feeds technician attendance directly
What is holding it
  • ORBIT: we need the interface specification and credentials
  • WhatsApp: a provider has to be chosen and paid for
  • Punch clock: we need the make and model of the machine

The business gain One number for the month that nobody had to reconcile by hand.

What these numbers assume
  • One developer on the platform throughout. Every figure scales with that. A second developer does not halve the total — but it does let Phase 2 run beside Phase 1, which pulls roughly two months out of the end date.
  • A mobile developer for the app. A promotional app is small enough that one person could do it after the floor work, but serially — that adds about four weeks to Phase 1 and stops everything else while it happens.
  • Decisions come back within a week of being asked. Each of the four open decisions sits directly in front of a phase. A month of silence on the team structure is a month added to Phase 1, not absorbed by it.
  • These are ranges, not commitments. The floor half of Phase 1 is accurate because most of it is built and measurable. The app is the least certain figure on the page — it is the only part with nothing built yet, and a promotional app can be four weeks or eight depending entirely on how much design and content it carries.

Four decisions we need from you

Each one gates a phase. Nothing else is waiting on anybody.

Who leads which team?

278 technicians and no team structure. We need the names of the team leaders and roughly who sits under each of them.

If it waits: Phase 1 is built but cannot be switched on. The Team Board would show every technician in the company on one screen.

Who owns the app store accounts, and who writes the content?

Pioneer needs its own Apple and Google developer accounts, registered to the company rather than to a person. A promotional app also needs something to promote: the services list, current offers, branch details and photographs.

Start the registration now. Business verification on the app stores takes weeks and cannot be sped up. It is the longest lead time on the whole roadmap and none of it is a coding task, so it should be running while the floor work is still being finished.

Which quality system?

There are two half-built quality paths in the platform from earlier work. We build on one and retire the other.

If it waits: Phase 2 cannot start. Building on both would double the cost and split the reporting.

Do managers see the floor?

Should the GM and Workshop Manager get the Team Board across all branches, or is it the team leader's screen only?

If it waits: Small — a settings change, not a rebuild. But it shapes what we build for reporting.

What could go wrong

Named honestly, with what we are doing about each.

RiskWhat it looks likeHow we handle it
Adoption on the floor Technicians do not start and stop jobs, so the time data is empty and the board lies. The technician screen is three buttons, on the phone. The team leader sees immediately who has not started.
Approval slows intake A customer who is standing at the counter now has to wait for an email. The advisor can approve on the customer's behalf at the counter. The link is for the ones who leave.
Changing status names mid-flight Thousands of live jobs use the old vocabulary. A half-finished rename breaks reports silently. Deliberately done in two steps. The new names were added without removing the old ones, so nothing can break in between.
Nobody installs the app A promotional app ships and becomes a marketing cost rather than a channel. The risk is higher than for a transactional app, because there is no job to check and no appointment to make — the reason to open it has to come from us. The install link goes out when the customer is already paying attention: with the job-closure message and the feedback survey. Offers are the reason to come back, so someone has to own keeping them current.
App store approval slips Apple or Google hold up the release and Phase 1 drifts by weeks. The account registration starts now, in parallel with the floor work, so the slow part is already done before there is anything to submit. A promotional app is also the easiest kind to get approved — no accounts, no payments, nothing for a reviewer to reject.
Third parties do not deliver ORBIT or the WhatsApp provider go quiet and Phase 3 stalls. Phase 3 is last on purpose. Phases 1 and 2 depend on nobody outside the business, apart from the app store registration.
